# The Next 3 Months (Months 2–4)

The 6 weeks gave you vocabulary and a framework. The next three months are about **depth, repetition,
and building real habits** — with a small, controlled introduction of real money on the investing
side only.

**Weekly time:** the same 2–3 hours, plus ~15 minutes on 2 weekdays for journalling/observation.

---

## Guiding principles for this phase

1. **Investing first, trading much later.** Investing compounds; trading is optional.
2. **Real money only where the downside is a slow drawdown, not a fast loss.**
3. **Everything gets written down.** Decisions, rationale, and outcomes.
4. **No leverage, no F&O, no tips.** These constraints stay.
5. **Boring is the objective.** If it feels exciting, you've probably moved into speculation.

## Month 2 — Consolidate and start (real money, small)

### Learning focus

| Topic | Resource |
|---|---|
| **Taxation of investments** — STCG, LTCG, STT, set-off, ITR reporting for capital gains | Zerodha Varsity Module 7: Markets & Taxation — https://zerodha.com/varsity/module/markets-and-taxation/ · Income Tax Dept — https://www.incometax.gov.in/ |
| **Choosing a broker** — total cost, platform stability, DP charges, complaint history | SEBI-registered brokers list — https://www.sebi.gov.in/intermediaries.html · NSE member list |
| **KYC, nomination, and account hygiene** | SEBI Investor site — https://investor.sebi.gov.in/ · CDSL/NSDL |
| **Personal finance foundations** — insurance, emergency fund, high-interest debt | Varsity Module 11 (Personal Finance) chapters on insurance and financial planning |

### Actions

- [ ] **Before any investing:** confirm you have (a) term insurance if you have dependants,
      (b) health insurance, (c) an emergency fund of 6 months' expenses, (d) no credit-card or
      personal-loan debt outstanding. If any is missing, fix that first. It beats any return you'll earn.
- [ ] Open a demat + trading account with a **SEBI-registered** broker. Verify the registration number.
- [ ] **Add nominees** to the demat account. People skip this and it causes years of family pain.
- [ ] Start a **small SIP** — an amount you would not notice losing 30% of — into **one broad, low-cost
      index fund**. Not five funds. One.
- [ ] Set up a spreadsheet that tracks: date, instrument, amount, NAV/price, rationale.
- [ ] Do **not** buy individual stocks yet.

### Exercise
Recompute the tax on a hypothetical set of transactions: three equity sales at different holding
periods, one debt fund sale, and one dividend receipt. Verify each rule against the Income Tax
Department site rather than a blog.

## Month 3 — Deepen analysis

### Learning focus

| Topic | Resource |
|---|---|
| **Sector-specific analysis** — how metrics differ across banks, IT, FMCG, pharma, autos | Varsity Module 3 later chapters; company annual reports; Screener |
| **Banking metrics** — NIM, GNPA/NNPA, CASA, provision coverage, capital adequacy | RBI Financial Stability Report — https://www.rbi.org.in/ ; any large bank's annual report |
| **Valuation depth** — DCF concepts, EV/EBITDA, when each multiple applies | Varsity Module 3 (equity research & DCF chapters); *Valuation* by McKinsey if ambitious |
| **Reading quarterly results** — the concall, the investor presentation, guidance | Company IR pages; NSE announcements |
| **Macro basics** — repo rate, inflation prints, GDP, why they matter for equities | RBI monetary policy statements; MoSPI releases |

### Actions

- [ ] Analyse **one company per week** (4 total) using a fixed template. Same template every time —
      that's what builds pattern recognition.
- [ ] Listen to **two earnings conference calls** end to end. Note what management dodges.
- [ ] Read **one full annual report** cover to cover. Yes, all of it, once. You'll never need to
      again, but you should know what's in there.
- [ ] Continue the SIP. Increase it only if income increased.
- [ ] Optional and small: if you want individual stocks, cap **all** direct equity at a small
      fraction of your portfolio, and each stock at ≤10% of that.

### Exercise
Build a **watchlist of 10 companies** with your analysis and a written "I would consider this at
price X because Y" note for each. Do not act on it. Revisit in Month 4 and see how your reasoning
held up.

## Month 4 — Systems, review, and (optionally) paper trading

### Learning focus

| Topic | Resource |
|---|---|
| **Building a trading system** — rules, backtesting concepts, sample size | Varsity Module 10: Trading Systems — https://zerodha.com/varsity/module/trading-systems/ |
| **Portfolio construction** — position limits, correlation, rebalancing rules | Varsity Module 9 (risk management chapters) |
| **Behavioural review** — reading your own journal for patterns | Your own journal |
| **Market history** — 2008, 2020, and Indian scams (Harshad Mehta, Ketan Parekh, Satyam, IL&FS) | *The Scam* — Debashis Basu & Sucheta Dalal; SEBI orders on the SEBI site |

### Actions

- [ ] Write a **formal Investment Policy Statement** (1–2 pages): goals, horizon, target allocation,
      what you will and won't buy, rebalancing trigger, review schedule.
- [ ] If you still want to trade: **paper trade one written strategy for the whole month**, minimum
      20 trades logged, with full plan and post-mortem for each. Compute expectancy **after
      realistic costs**. If expectancy is negative, you have your answer, and it cost you nothing.
- [ ] **Quarterly portfolio review**: performance vs benchmark, allocation drift, rationale check.
- [ ] Re-read your Week 5 rulebook and update it based on what you actually did.

### Exercise
Read about **one historical market failure** (a scam, a bubble, or a collapse) and write 300 words
on the specific warning signs that were visible beforehand — and whether you would have spotted them
with the tools you now have. Be honest; the answer is usually "no", and that humility is the point.

## Month-by-month summary

| | Month 2 | Month 3 | Month 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Theme** | Foundations + first real SIP | Analytical depth | Systems & review |
| **Real money** | Small SIP in one index fund | Continue SIP; optional tiny direct equity | Continue; formalise policy |

| **Output** | Tracking sheet + tax understanding | 4 company analyses + watchlist | IPS + expectancy report + review |

## Milestones — you're on track if, by end of Month 4, you have

- [ ] Insurance, emergency fund, and no high-interest debt — in place *before* investing
- [ ] A running SIP in one low-cost broad index fund, with nominees registered
- [ ] A tracking spreadsheet with dated entries and written rationale
- [ ] Four completed company analyses using one consistent template
- [ ] A 10-company watchlist with entry reasoning
- [ ] A written Investment Policy Statement
- [ ] A journal you have actually re-read and learned something from
- [ ] Zero rupees lost to leverage, F&O, or a tip

---

## Months 5–12: where this goes next

Once the above is habitual:

- **If you're leaning long-term investing:** deepen valuation, study business models and moats,
  read a company's 10 years of annual reports, learn sector economics, and raise your SIP with income.
  Add complexity slowly: mid-cap exposure, international diversification, debt allocation.
- **If you're leaning trading:** spend 6+ months paper trading and refining one system before any
  real capital, then start with an amount whose total loss would be irrelevant to your life.
  Study execution, costs, and statistics — not more indicators.
- **If you're leaning both:** keep the two accounts, the two journals, and the two capital pools
  **completely separate**. Never fund trading losses from the investing pot. This one rule prevents
  most of the damage people do to themselves.
