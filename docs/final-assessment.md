# Final Practical Assessment

Take this at the end of Week 6. Set aside **2 hours**, work without notes for Part A, and use
real data for Parts B–E.

**Pass mark: 70% overall — and you must not fail Part D (risk).** Risk management is the section
where a failing score has real consequences.

---

## Part A — Concepts (20 marks, no notes)

Answer in one or two sentences each. 2 marks per question.

1. Explain what you actually own when you own a share.
2. You buy shares on the NSE. How much of your money reaches the company, and why?
3. Explain the difference between CFO and net profit, and why the gap matters.
4. Why is a P/E of 8 not automatically "cheap"?
5. Fund A has NAV ₹12, Fund B has NAV ₹380. Which is better value, and why is the question flawed?
6. Explain real return using an FD at 7%, inflation at 6%, and a 30% tax slab.
7. Why does a wider stop loss result in a *smaller* position size?
8. Your portfolio is down 50%. What return do you need to recover, and what does that imply about risk management?
9. Explain why an option buyer can be right about direction and still lose 100%.
10. Distinguish trading from speculation in one sentence each.

---

## Part B — Company analysis (25 marks)

Pick any Indian listed company you have **not** analysed before. Use Screener.in and the company's
latest annual report from the NSE/BSE site.

Produce a **one-page** write-up containing:

| Requirement | Marks |
|---|---|
| Business description in 3 sentences: what it sells, to whom, how it makes money | 4 |
| 5-year revenue and profit CAGR, with the numbers shown | 3 |
| Operating margin and net margin trend over 5 years, with a comment on direction | 4 |
| ROE and ROCE (3-year average), with a comment on which is more meaningful here and why | 4 |
| Debt/equity and interest coverage, with a solvency comment | 3 |
| **CFO vs net profit over 5 years** — table and verdict | 4 |
| Two genuine red flags or open questions you found (pledge, contingent liability, receivables trend, auditor note, related-party transactions, dilution) | 3 |

**Automatic deduction:** using standalone instead of consolidated statements (−3).

⚠️ This is an *analysis exercise*. It is not a buy decision, and you are not buying anything.

---

## Part C — Chart analysis (15 marks)

Pick a different Nifty 50 stock. Use a **daily** chart on TradingView with at least 1 year of data.

| Requirement | Marks |
|---|---|
| State the regime (uptrend/downtrend/range) with evidence in terms of highs and lows | 3 |
| Mark two support zones and two resistance zones (screenshot or sketch) | 4 |
| Identify one past breakout and one past false breakout; comment on the volume in each case | 4 |
| Position of price relative to the 50-EMA and 200-EMA, and what that implies | 2 |
| One instance where RSI exceeded 70 and price continued higher — and what you conclude | 2 |

## Part D — Risk management (25 marks) — **must pass**

Show your working.

1. **(5)** Capital ₹6,00,000, risk per trade 1%. Entry ₹840, stop ₹805. Calculate the position size, the capital deployed, and the deployed capital as a % of the portfolio.
2. **(4)** For the same trade, the target is ₹945. Calculate the risk/reward. Would you take it under a 1:2 minimum rule?
3. **(4)** Build the loss/recovery table for −10%, −25%, −50%, −75%. Show the formula.
4. **(4)** You have ₹2,00,000 and use 5× leverage. What adverse % move wipes out your capital? What happens operationally before that point?
5. **(4)** You have six open positions, each risking 1%, and four of them are private-sector banks. State two separate problems with this portfolio.
6. **(4)** Write your personal circuit-breaker rules: what makes you stop for the day, for the week, and for the month.

**Fail conditions for Part D:** answering Q1 by choosing a share quantity first, or Q6 with "I'd
stop when I feel like it".

## Part E — Portfolio review & self-assessment (15 marks)

Return to the mock portfolio you created in Week 2.

| Requirement | Marks |
|---|---|
| Current value vs cost, per holding and overall (absolute % and, if you like, annualised) | 3 |
| For each holding, was the original written rationale correct so far? Be honest | 4 |
| One holding you would now remove, and the *analytical* reason (not "it went down") | 3 |
| Your asset allocation now vs your target — is a rebalance due? | 2 |
| Three specific mistakes you made during the 6 weeks (in reasoning, not in outcome) | 3 |

## Scoring

| Score | Interpretation | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **85–100** | Strong foundation | Proceed to the [3-month roadmap](next-3-months-roadmap.md). Consider starting small real investments in a broad index fund — *after* the emergency fund. |
| **70–84** | Solid, with gaps | Identify weak sections, redo those weeks' exercises, then proceed. |
| **55–69** | Partial understanding | Repeat Weeks 3 and 5 before doing anything with real money. |
| **Below 55** | Not ready | Repeat the full 6 weeks. There's no time pressure and no cost to being thorough. |
| **Failed Part D** | Stop | Regardless of total score, redo Week 5 completely. Everything else is optional; this isn't. |

---

## Honesty clauses

Answer these to yourself, in writing:

1. Did you actually do the exercises, or did you read the material and assume you'd absorbed it?
2. Did you log the trades you got **wrong**, or only the ones you got right?
3. Have you already, at any point in these 6 weeks, put real money into something you didn't understand?
4. Did you follow a tip from anyone during this period?

Answering (3) or (4) with "yes" means the risk-management module hasn't taken hold yet.
That's fixable — but only if you're honest about it now rather than after it costs you.
