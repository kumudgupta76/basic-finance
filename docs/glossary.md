# Beginner's Glossary — Indian Stock Market

Format: **Term** — plain explanation. *Example where useful.*

---

## A

**AMC (Asset Management Company)** — The company that runs mutual fund schemes. *e.g. the fund house whose name appears before the scheme name.*

**AMFI** — Association of Mutual Funds in India; the industry body. Publishes NAVs and investor education material.

**Amortisation** — Depreciation, but for intangible assets (patents, software, goodwill).

**Anchoring** — Fixating on an irrelevant reference price, usually what *you* paid. The market doesn't know your cost.

**Annual report** — A company's yearly disclosure document: statements, MD&A, auditor's report, notes.

**Arbitrage** — Profiting from the same asset being priced differently in two places. Keeps NSE and BSE prices aligned.

**Ask (Offer)** — The lowest price a seller is currently willing to accept.

**Asset allocation** — How your money is split across asset classes (equity/debt/gold/cash). Drives most of your long-run outcome.

**ATM (At The Money)** — An option whose strike is roughly equal to the current price.

**ATR (Average True Range)** — Average size of a stock's daily range. Used to set stops outside normal noise.

**AUM (Assets Under Management)** — Total money a fund or manager handles.

## B

**Balance sheet** — Snapshot of what a company owns and owes at a point in time. Assets = Liabilities + Equity.

**Bear market** — A sustained decline, commonly 20%+ from the peak.

**Bid** — The highest price a buyer is currently willing to pay.

**Bonus issue** — Free extra shares to existing holders. *1:1 bonus doubles your shares and halves the price — your value is unchanged.*

**Book value** — Net worth: assets minus liabilities. The accounting value of shareholders' claim.

**Breakout** — Price moving decisively past a support or resistance zone.

**Broker** — SEBI-registered intermediary that routes your orders to the exchange.

**BSE** — Bombay Stock Exchange, established 1875. Benchmark index: Sensex.

**Buyback** — A company repurchasing its own shares, reducing the share count.

**Bull market** — A sustained period of rising prices.

## C

**CAGR** — Compound Annual Growth Rate; the smoothed annual rate between a start and end value. *₹1L → ₹2L in 6 years ≈ 12.2% CAGR.*

**Call option** — Right (not obligation) for the buyer to **buy** the underlying at the strike price.

**Candlestick** — A chart glyph showing open, high, low, close for one period.

**Capex (Capital expenditure)** — Money spent on long-lived assets like plant and machinery.

**CDSL / NSDL** — India's two depositories; they hold your shares electronically.

**CFO / CFI / CFF** — Cash flow from Operating / Investing / Financing activities.

**Circuit limit** — Exchange-imposed price band that halts or restricts trading after a large move.

**Confirmation bias** — Only seeking evidence that supports the view you already hold.

**Consolidated statements** — Financials including subsidiaries and associates. Always prefer these over standalone.

**Contingent liability** — A potential obligation depending on a future event (litigation, guarantees, tax disputes). Found in the notes.

**Corporate action** — Company event affecting shares: dividend, bonus, split, rights, buyback.

**Correction** — A fall of roughly 10% from a recent high. Common and normal.

**Correlation** — The degree to which two holdings move together. High correlation destroys diversification.

## D

**Death cross** — 50-day moving average crossing below the 200-day. Heavily lagging; over-hyped.

**Delta** — How much an option's price moves for a ₹1 move in the underlying.

**Demat account** — Account that holds your shares electronically.

**Depository Participant (DP)** — Your interface to NSDL/CDSL; usually your broker.

**Depreciation** — Spreading an asset's cost over its useful life. A **non-cash** expense.

**Derivative** — A contract deriving its value from an underlying asset. Futures and options are derivatives.

**Dilution** — Increase in share count (QIP, ESOPs, conversions) that reduces EPS.

**Direct plan** — Mutual fund plan bought straight from the AMC, with no distributor commission → lower expense ratio.

**Disposition effect** — Selling winners too early and holding losers too long.

**Diversification** — Spreading money across holdings that don't fail together. Handles company risk, not market risk.

**Dividend** — Cash distributed to shareholders out of profits.

**Dividend yield** — Annual dividend per share ÷ price.

**Doji** — Candlestick with open ≈ close. Indecision.

**Drawdown** — Peak-to-trough decline in portfolio value.

**D/E (Debt to Equity)** — Total debt ÷ shareholders' equity. Doesn't apply the same way to financial companies.

## E

**EBIT** — Earnings Before Interest and Tax; operating profit.

**EBITDA** — EBIT before depreciation and amortisation. **Not** cash flow.

**EMA** — Exponential Moving Average; weights recent prices more heavily than an SMA.

**EPS (Earnings Per Share)** — Net profit ÷ number of shares.

**Equity** — Ownership; also, shareholders' funds on the balance sheet.

**ETF (Exchange Traded Fund)** — A fund whose units trade on the exchange like a share.

**Ex-date** — The cut-off date from which a buyer no longer receives the announced corporate benefit.

**Expectancy** — (Win% × Avg win) − (Loss% × Avg loss). Tells you if a strategy makes money over many trades.

**Expense ratio (TER)** — Annual fund fee as a % of assets, deducted from NAV.

**Expiry** — The date a derivative contract ceases to exist.

**Exposure margin** — Additional margin over SPAN margin for derivatives positions.

## F

**False breakout (whipsaw)** — A breakout that immediately reverses, trapping entrants.

**FCF (Free Cash Flow)** — CFO minus capex. Cash genuinely available to capital providers.

**FOMO** — Fear Of Missing Out; buying because something is already moving.

**FPO** — Follow-on Public Offer; an additional share sale by an already-listed company.

**Free float** — Shares actually available for public trading, excluding locked-in promoter holdings.

**Futures** — A standardised contract to buy/sell an asset at a set price on a future date. Leveraged and symmetric.

## G

**Gamma** — The rate at which Delta changes. Largest for ATM options near expiry.

**Golden cross** — 50-day MA crossing above the 200-day. Lagging; treat as context, not signal.

**Gross margin** — Gross profit ÷ revenue.

**GST** — Goods and Services Tax; applies to brokerage and some transaction charges.

## H

**Hammer** — Candle with a small body and long lower wick after a decline; possible bottoming attempt.

**Heat (portfolio heat)** — Total risk across all open positions combined.

**Hindsight bias** — Believing past chart moves were obvious. They weren't, in real time.

## I

**Index** — A basket of stocks tracked as a single number. *Nifty 50, Sensex.*

**Index fund** — A fund that mechanically replicates an index instead of trying to beat it.

**India VIX** — NSE's volatility index, derived from Nifty options. The market's "fear gauge".

**Inflation** — The rate at which prices rise and money loses purchasing power.

**Interest coverage** — EBIT ÷ interest expense. How many times over the company can pay its interest.

**Intraday** — Positions opened and closed the same day.

**Intrinsic value (options)** — The exercise value right now. Never negative.

**Inventory** — Unsold goods and raw materials.

**IPO** — Initial Public Offering; a company's first sale of shares to the public.

**ITM (In The Money)** — An option that already has exercise value.

**IV (Implied Volatility)** — Expected future volatility backed out of an option's price.

**IV crush** — The collapse in implied volatility right after a known event, which can cause option buyers to lose despite being directionally right.

## L

**Large cap** — Companies ranked 1–100 by market capitalisation (SEBI classification).

**Leverage** — Controlling a larger position than your own capital allows. Magnifies both gains and losses.

**Limit order** — An order that executes only at your specified price or better.

**Liquidity** — How easily you can buy/sell size without moving the price.

**Lot size** — The fixed quantity in one derivatives contract.

**LTP (Last Traded Price)** — The price of the most recent trade.

## M

**MACD** — Momentum indicator: 12-EMA minus 26-EMA, with a 9-EMA signal line and a histogram.

**Margin** — Collateral required to hold a leveraged position.

**Margin call** — A demand to deposit more funds when a leveraged position moves against you.

**Market capitalisation** — Share price × number of shares. The company's size.

**Market order** — Executes immediately at the best available price. You don't control the price.

**Mark-to-market (MTM)** — Daily cash settlement of derivative gains/losses.

**MD&A** — Management Discussion & Analysis section of an annual report.

**Mid cap** — Companies ranked 101–250 by market capitalisation.

**Moneyness** — Whether an option is ITM, ATM, or OTM.

**MTF (Margin Trading Facility)** — Broker-provided leverage for delivery trades.

## N

**NAV (Net Asset Value)** — Per-unit value of a mutual fund. A low NAV is **not** cheap.

**Net margin** — Net profit ÷ revenue.

**Net worth** — Assets minus liabilities. Same as shareholders' equity / book value.

**Nifty 50** — NSE's benchmark index of 50 large, liquid companies, free-float weighted.

**Notional value** — Contract price × lot size. Your real derivative exposure.

**NSE** — National Stock Exchange, India's largest exchange by volume.

## O

**OHLC** — Open, High, Low, Close.

**Open Interest (OI)** — Number of derivative contracts currently outstanding.

**Operating margin** — EBIT ÷ revenue.

**Option** — A contract giving the buyer a right (not obligation) to buy (call) or sell (put) at a strike price.

**Option chain** — Table of all strikes with their call and put prices, OI, and IV.

**OTM (Out of The Money)** — An option with zero intrinsic value; 100% time value.

**Other income** — Income not from the core business. Watch how much of profit it represents.

**Overtrading** — Trading far more than your edge or attention justifies.

## P

**PAT** — Profit After Tax; net profit; the bottom line.

**P/B (Price to Book)** — Market cap ÷ net worth. Most useful for financials.

**PBT** — Profit Before Tax.

**P/E (Price to Earnings)** — Price ÷ EPS. Comparable only within a sector.

**PEG** — P/E ÷ growth rate. A crude check on whether a high P/E is justified.

**Physical settlement** — Settlement by actual delivery of shares. Indian **stock** F&O is physically settled.

**Position sizing** — Deciding how many shares to buy based on your risk limit and stop distance.

**Premium (options)** — The price paid by the buyer to the seller. The buyer's maximum loss.

**Primary market** — Where the company itself issues shares (IPO/FPO/rights). Money goes to the company.

**Promoter** — Founding/controlling shareholder group.

**Promoter pledge** — Promoter shares pledged as loan collateral. A known risk factor.

**Put option** — Right for the buyer to **sell** the underlying at the strike price.

## Q

**Qualified opinion** — An auditor's report that is not clean. A serious red flag.

## R

**Rebalancing** — Restoring your target asset allocation by trimming what grew and adding to what lagged.

**Real return** — Return after inflation. The only return that matters.

**Receivables (debtors)** — Money owed to the company by customers.

**Record date** — Date on which the company checks its register to decide eligibility for a corporate action.

**Regular plan** — Mutual fund plan bought through a distributor; higher expense ratio than Direct.

**Related-party transaction** — Business done with entities connected to promoters/management. Read these.

**Resistance** — A price zone where selling has repeatedly stopped advances.

**Revenge trading** — Taking an unplanned, oversized trade to recover a loss. The most destructive retail behaviour.

**Rights issue** — Offer of new shares to existing shareholders at a set price.

**Risk/reward (R:R)** — Potential gain ÷ potential loss on a trade. Reject below 1:2 as a beginner.

**Role reversal (polarity)** — Broken resistance often becomes support, and vice versa.

**ROCE** — EBIT ÷ (equity + debt). Return on all capital; not distorted by leverage.

**ROE** — Net profit ÷ shareholders' equity. Can be inflated by debt.

**Rollover** — Moving a derivatives position from the expiring contract to the next one.

**RSI** — Relative Strength Index; momentum oscillator from 0–100. Overbought ≠ sell.

**RTA (Registrar & Transfer Agent)** — Maintains shareholder records on behalf of companies.

**Rupee cost averaging** — Buying more units when prices are low via fixed periodic investment (SIP).

## S

**SEBI** — Securities and Exchange Board of India; the market regulator.

**SCORES** — SEBI's online investor grievance redressal system.

**Secondary market** — Investor-to-investor trading on the exchange. The company receives nothing.

**Sensex** — BSE's 30-stock benchmark index.

**Settlement** — The actual exchange of shares and money after a trade.

**SIP (Systematic Investment Plan)** — Investing a fixed amount at fixed intervals. A method, not a product.

**Slippage** — The difference between the price you expected and the price you got.

**Small cap** — Companies ranked 251 and beyond by market capitalisation.

**SMA** — Simple Moving Average.

**SPAN margin** — Risk-based margin for derivatives, calculated by the exchange's model.

**Speculation** — Taking a position on price based on a narrative rather than cash flows or a tested edge.

**Split (stock split)** — Reducing face value so each share becomes several. Total value unchanged.

**Spread** — Ask minus bid. A hidden trading cost.

**Standalone statements** — Financials of the parent company only. Use consolidated instead.

**Step-up SIP** — A SIP whose amount increases each year.

**Stop loss** — A pre-decided exit price for a losing position. Non-negotiable.

**STT (Securities Transaction Tax)** — Tax on securities transactions; a meaningful cost for active traders.

**Sunk cost fallacy** — Adding to a loser purely because you're already in it.

**Support** — A price zone where buying has repeatedly stopped declines.

## T

**T+1** — Settlement on the working day after the trade. Verify the current cycle on NSE/SEBI.

**TER** — Total Expense Ratio. See *Expense ratio*.

**Theta** — Time decay. Negative for option buyers, positive for sellers.

**Time value (extrinsic value)** — Premium minus intrinsic value. Decays to zero at expiry.

**Timeframe** — The period each candle represents.

**Total return / TRI** — Return including dividends. Funds must benchmark against the Total Return Index.

**Tracking error** — How far an index fund's returns deviate from its index.

**Trading account** — The account through which orders are placed.

**Trend** — A sequence of higher highs and higher lows (up) or lower highs and lower lows (down).

**TTM (Trailing Twelve Months)** — The last 12 months of reported figures.

## U

**Underlying** — The asset a derivative derives its value from.

**Upper/lower circuit** — Price band limits set by the exchange.

## V

**Vega** — Sensitivity of an option's price to changes in implied volatility.

**Volatility** — How much and how fast prices swing.

**Volume** — Number of shares traded in a period. Compare to its own 20-day average.

## W

**Working capital** — Current assets minus current liabilities; money tied up in operations.

**Writer (option writer)** — The option seller. Limited gain, very large potential loss.
