# Concepts You Should Know After 6 Weeks

Tick each item only if you can **explain it out loud to someone else** in plain language, with an
example. Recognising a term is not the same as understanding it.

---

## 1. Market structure & mechanics

- [ ] What a share actually represents (part-ownership of a business)
- [ ] The difference between NSE and BSE
- [ ] What Nifty 50 and Sensex measure, and what they exclude
- [ ] Free-float market capitalisation weighting
- [ ] Primary market vs secondary market — and where your money goes in each
- [ ] What an IPO is, and why it's a hard thing for a beginner to analyse
- [ ] Roles of: SEBI, exchange, clearing corporation, broker, DP, NSDL/CDSL, RTA
- [ ] Demat account vs trading account vs bank account
- [ ] Bid, ask, spread, and what a wide spread tells you
- [ ] Market order vs limit order vs stop-loss order — and when each is dangerous
- [ ] Market capitalisation, and why share price alone says nothing about size
- [ ] Large cap / mid cap / small cap classification
- [ ] Volume vs liquidity, and the risk of being trapped in an illiquid stock
- [ ] Bull market, bear market, correction
- [ ] Settlement cycle (and that it has changed and may change again)
- [ ] Corporate actions: dividend, bonus, split, rights, buyback
- [ ] Ex-date vs record date, and why buying "for the dividend" late doesn't work
- [ ] Why a bonus issue or split does not make you richer

- [ ] Compounding, and why the last decade matters most

- [ ] Matching time horizon to asset class
- [ ] Why an emergency fund comes before investing
- [ ] Diversification — what it protects against and what it does not

- [ ] Tracking error

- [ ] Margin, margin call, forced square-off

- [ ] Position limits and sector limits
- [ ] Correlation, and why four bank stocks are one bet
- [ ] FOMO

- [ ] Overtrading, and why costs scale with activity but edge does not

- [ ] ITM / ATM / OTM

- [ ] Protective put and covered call, conceptually

## Meta-skills you should also have

- [ ] You can find a company's annual report on the NSE/BSE site without Googling a blog.
- [ ] You can pull 10 years of financials for any Indian listed company in under two minutes.
- [ ] You can verify whether an intermediary is SEBI-registered.
- [ ] You know where to file a complaint (SEBI SCORES).
- [ ] You can spot a "guaranteed returns" pitch as fraud instantly.
- [ ] You have a written rulebook, and you have re-read it.
- [ ] You have a mock portfolio with dated entries and written rationale.
- [ ] You have a trade journal with at least a few honestly-recorded losers.

---

**Scoring yourself:** if more than 20 boxes are unticked, don't move on — revisit the relevant week.
The material compounds; gaps in Week 2 make Week 5 useless.
